# Blog hero image specs — 8 bespoke replacements

_Created 2026-10-19. Status: **specced, not generated.** Generation needs sign-off (API spend)._

## Why

The blog is 26 posts sharing **14 recycled service-page heroes**. `/child-custody-hero.webp`
carries four posts, `/profilin-hero.webp` three. On the blog index grid that reads as a content
farm, which undercuts the premium positioning the $375 rate is doing work to establish.
`GATRUTH.webp` is also square (1024x1024) and crops badly as a social card.

These 8 break up both duplicate clusters and fix the wrong-shaped image.

## Style sheet — reuse this to brief any future image in the set

> G.A. Tucker PI hero images are cinematic stills, not illustrations: near-black environments
> (#0D0D0D–#1A1A1A) lit by exactly one warm practical source (amber #CEA53D — a desk lamp, a
> streetlamp, light through blinds) against cool blue-grey ambient shadow, shot with a shallow-
> depth-of-field anamorphic-adjacent lens look (sharp foreground object, soft bokeh background,
> often city-lights-through-blinds). The subject is always an object, never a person's face —
> when a human presence is implied it is a silhouette, a turned back, hands in shadow, or simply
> absent and inferred through objects (an empty chair, a toy on a step). No readable text,
> documents, logos, badges, seals, or law-enforcement signifiers ever appear — everything
> legal-adjacent must read as private, licensed, and lawful, never as police or court. Compose
> with the primary subject in the right-of-center third, outer 10% of frame kept empty, and the
> left two-thirds low-detail and dark enough to carry a large white headline in the site's
> overlay. Full-bleed 16:9 only — no letterboxing. Palette stays black / warm gold / off-white
> #EDEDED with occasional cool grey-blue for contrast; grain and color grade should read like a
> 35mm film still, not a glossy render.

## Specs

**All:** 1920x1080, 16:9, WebP. Must survive a center crop to 1.91:1 (social card) and to a
square-ish grid thumbnail. Must work as a darkened backdrop under large white headline text —
keep the left two-thirds low-detail.

## How the set varies

Deliberately spread so the blog index grid does not read as repeated thumbnails:

| | Camera | Environment | Light source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | eye-level, wide | exterior driveway | warm window glow |
| 2 | eye-level, cabin | vehicle interior | dashboard glow + passing headlights |
| 3 | eye-level | porch at dusk | warm porch lamp |
| 4 | **low angle, looking up** | industrial stairwell | bare bulb / fluorescent |
| 5 | **true overhead / plan** | entryway floor | hard overhead fixture |
| 6 | tabletop | dark table | **cold phone-screen glow** |
| 7 | eye-level | conference table | warm desk lamp |
| 8 | eye-level | boardroom | warm pendant |

Six distinct settings, eight distinct light treatments, only one warm-lamp desk close-up.

## Hard constraints baked into every prompt

1. No identifiable faces. No people at all in the final set.
2. **No children's faces, ever.** #3 implies a child only through a toy on a step.
3. No readable documents, names, addresses, or dates.
4. No badges, seals, or law-enforcement signifiers. He is a PI, not police.
5. **No surveillance-voyeur framing.** #2 explicitly faces down an open street, never toward a
   residence — the whole blog position is that the work stays inside the law.
6. No trench coats, fedoras, magnifying glasses, fingerprint overlays, or hacker aesthetics.

## Open questions before generating

- **Zero human figures across all eight** is a conservative read of the no-faces rule. The
  existing 18 images do use silhouettes and turned backs, so this set will read as stylistically
  distinct. Greg's call.
- **Cost:** ~$0.02–0.04 per generation, 3–5 attempts per final image, so roughly **$1–2 total**.
  Fine mechanical detail (cane, pen, railings) is where this model class glitches; #3 and #5
  carry the highest regeneration risk.
- Generate at the nearest native supported ratio and upscale to 1920x1080 — do **not** let the
  model pad to 16:9, which produces the letterboxing defect being fixed.
